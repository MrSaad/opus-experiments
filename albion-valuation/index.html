1625 Albion Road: what should we pay?

This is an independent valuation of the two-storey plus basement commercial building at the southwest corner of Albion Road and Martin Grove Road. It is built from the owner's rent roll and 2025 operating statement, cross-checked against the draft third-party appraisal and current market evidence I found online. The first-glance evaluation was read for reference only; none of its numbers are used in the model.

Valuation date: October 1, 2026. All figures are CAD. This is an investment analysis, not a CUSPAP appraisal, and it relies on unaudited owner documents. Verify the items in the diligence list before you firm up an offer.

Why the value is much lower than the ask

  • Only about 60% of the space produces rent. Of 24,984 sf of leasable area, 9,563 sf is vacant and 623 sf is used by the owner. The second floor is 69% vacant.
  • Today's building nets about $205K a year once costs are normalized: $506K of gross rent, minus about $278K of real operating costs, minus a credit allowance. At $7.0M, that is a 2.9% yield.
  • A full building nets only about $343K a year, because the vacant space is mostly second-floor office and basement. That space rents for $17–22/sf gross, not the $40+ the main floor earns.
  • Getting there costs about $800K: lost rent while leasing, tenant improvements, commissions, a roof and HVAC allowance, and a return for taking the risk.
  • The right cap rate is about 7.0%, a blend of 6.0% for main-floor retail, 8.5% for suburban office and 9.25% for basement space. The appraisal used 5.0%, which is below the published Toronto ranges for every component.

What would make it worth more

  • Lease the second floor to medical users at about $26/sf gross within a year. It is built out, Etobicoke General Hospital is 1.4 km away, and the Finch West LRT opened in Dec 2025. In the upside case the as-is value rises to about $5.2M.
  • Check whether the $17/sf "additional rent" is contractual. If it is fixed, the main-floor rents hold. If leases say "proportionate share", tenants are overpaying by about $6.60/sf, which is roughly $86K a year of income at risk.
  • Renewals are moving in the right direction. Four main-floor tenants, including the dentist and physio (both here since 2003), renewed in 2025 through 2030. That is real evidence for main-floor rents.
  • Your intended use (seniors care) needs rezoning. Don't pay for it up front. Make it upside you capture after closing.

3 · Income & operating costs

The owner's statement omits insurance and reserves; normalized in-place NOI is about $205K

Owner's 2025 operating statement

Normalized in-place NOI (buyer's view, today's occupancy)

Credit allowance: 3% on leased income plus 25% on month-to-month cash income. Management is re-priced at 4% of EGI, an arm's-length rate, in place of the $25,000 related-party fee. Insurance is my estimate (about $0.74/sf GFA), because none was shown. R&M is $0.60/sf, in place of the $10,000 shown. A $0.40/sf structural reserve is added.

The $17/sf "additional rent" is the biggest open question in the income

Every formal lease pays a flat $17.00/sf of additional rent ($221,136/yr). Actual costs, including insurance, are about $259K, which is $10.37/sf across the 24,984 sf building. Today the leased tenants are effectively carrying the cost of the vacant space too.

  • If the leases fix additional rent at $17 (semi-gross), the income holds, and recent 2025 renewals at $41–46/sf gross show tenants accept that total cost.
  • If the leases say "proportionate share of operating costs", tenants could audit and claim about $6.60/sf × 13,008 sf ≈ $86K a year. That would cut in-place NOI by over 40%.

To stay neutral to this, I value on gross rents (base + additional) against all costs. Read every lease's additional-rent clause before waiving conditions.

Selected cap rates

I capitalize each income stream at its own rate, then blend by value:

  • Main-floor retail at 6.0%: mid neighbourhood range. Local independent tenants, unanchored, Rexdale, but long-tenured medical and service uses.
  • Second-floor office at 8.5%: suburban Class B range, small suites, not an office node.
  • Basement at 9.25%: below grade, small storage and service users, weakest covenant.

The blended rate is 7.0%. The appraisal's 5.0% came from strata unit sales such as single units on Terry Fox Way, Bloor St W and Highway 7. Owner-users buy those, and they price differently from a multi-tenant investment building.

Sales evidence

The appraisal's four sales were all 100% leased. Most are urban main-street buildings with apartments upstairs. The most relevant local benchmark is 964-1010 Albion Rd, a few kilometres east on Albion Rd: 93% leased, 35,230 sf, $1.01M NOI at $29.24/sf net rents, on a 1.8-acre site with a Mixed Use designation that makes it a redevelopment play. It was asked at $20M (5.1% cap, $568/sf) and is now sold conditionally. Even fully leased, the subject would earn about half that NOI per sf ($13.70/sf of leasable area vs $28.80), and its Neighbourhoods designation gives far less redevelopment upside. Price per square foot does not transfer between the two.

Market context

Rates. The Bank of Canada held at 2.25% on Sept 2, 2026. The 5-year GoC yield is about 3.70%. Conventional commercial mortgages run about 5.5–6.5%; I use 6.0% amortized over 25 years.

Issues found

  1. It values a 38–59% vacant building as if stabilized, with no lease-up deduction. It assumes 4% vacancy (citing 1.9% CoStar GTA North vacancy), yet its own CoStar page shows subject rents down 14% year-over-year and peer rents forecast to fall. No downtime, TI, commissions or risk are deducted.
  2. The cap rate is below every published range it cites. 5.0% is below Colliers' neighbourhood/strip retail range (5.5–6.5%) and suburban office (6.5–9.0%) on its own p.68. The cap-rate comps are small strata units, plus a Yonge St building and a Bloor St W unit.
  3. Its own sales comps imply higher cap rates. The sales averaged a 5.6% cap (6.7% Junction, 5.5% St Clair, 4.6% for a power-of-sale) on fully leased buildings. Even unadjusted, that is above the 5.0% chosen.
  4. Operating costs are understated by about $60K. Utilities are $30K against $60.5K actual (about $73K at full occupancy). Cleaning, snow, landscaping, waste, elevator and fire (about $41K) are not shown. Insurance at $15K is light. R&M at $51.7K partly offsets this.
  5. Office and basement rents exceed the local evidence. Office is at $24.65 gross against an $8 net asking rent across the street and the subject's own $12 net asking. Basement is at $22.65 gross against the $18 gross the 2025 basement leases achieved.
  6. The direct comparison applies $380/sf to 25,874 sf. That size matches neither the 32,278 sf GFA nor the 24,984 sf leasable. It also applies one rate to basement and above-grade space and makes no vacancy adjustment.
  7. The rent roll differs from the owner's (see section 2), and comp 3's $/sf was "not provided… should be inserted". The draft is unsigned and the reconciliation calls $9.33M the "midpoint… consistent with the income approach", but the income approach was $8.83M.

Not every flaw cuts against the appraisal. Its $195,282 TMI recovery figure ($7.65/sf) is below the $221,136 tenants actually pay today. My gross-rent method sidesteps that issue.

8 · First-glance evaluation (May 17, 2026), used as reference only

Its direction is right; some specifics needed correcting

Where I agree

  • The appraisal's 5.0% cap, 4% vacancy and $17 net office rent are not supported.
  • The office market across the street ($8 net) is the right reality check.
  • The pharmacy expiry (2026), cash tenants and related-party management are real diligence items.
  • Retirement or assisted-living use is not permitted under CL zoning and needs rezoning. That is upside to capture, not something to pay for.

Where I differ

  • "$1/sf basement deals" are not below market. With $17 additional rent they pay $18 gross, which is market for basement space and in line with the cash tenants.
  • Capitalizing in-place NOI at 7–8.5% without crediting lease-up double-counts the vacancy. The upside of leasing has real value, as long as the leasing costs are deducted.
  • Its $4.0–5.5M range is a bit high at the top. Its "stabilized" case puts all space at 7–8.5%. When main-floor retail (about 60% of value) is capped at its own rate and lease-up costs are deducted, I get $3.7–4.6M.
  • Its demographics table looks wrong. Unemployment of 28–38% is not plausible, and I did not rely on it. It does not mention the Finch West LRT, and it predates the September 2026 relisting at $6.999M.
9 · Offer strategy, risks & due diligence

Offer $3.5M, settle at $3.75–3.9M, walk away above $4.1M

The walk-away price equals my fair-value estimate, because paying more means paying for the seller's upside. The target range is where the 10-year unlevered return clears about 8% after roughly $200K of land transfer tax and closing costs. The opening offer is close to a 9% return. If the seller delivers signed second-floor leases or the pharmacy renewal before closing, the upside case supports stepping up to about $4.4M.

"All-in equity" = price + land transfer tax (Ontario + Toronto MLTT, 2% each above $400K) + about $60K legal and diligence − loan + about $429K of lease-up TI, commissions and capital. The loan is sized at 1.25× DSCR on today's normalized NOI ($205K), 6.0%, 25-year amortization, which caps it at about $2.1M at any price. The IRR is unlevered over 10 years, after closing costs, under base assumptions. *Under the upside case (second floor leased at $26 gross within 12 months), a $4.4M price returns about 8.8%.

Negotiating points

  • The ask needs a 4.9% cap on a full building, before any leasing cost. Today that is a 2.9% yield on normalized NOI.
  • The property has been on the market since April 2026 and was relisted with a price in September, which suggests there are few takers at the seller's expectations.
  • The seller bought it in 2011 for $2.48M, so $3.9M is still a 57% gain for them.
  • Debt only covers about $2.1M at today's income. Any buyer at $7M would need about $5M of cash for a 2.9% yield.
  • Offer to pay more for proven income: a price step-up (for example +$150–250K) if, before closing, signed second-floor leases at ≥$22 gross or a pharmacy renewal are delivered.

Conditions to put in the offer

  • 60–90 day due diligence. Financing condition sized on in-place NOI.
  • Certified rent roll and tenant estoppels from every occupant, including the cash tenants.
  • Copies of all leases, amendments, renewal notices and arrears ledger. Two years of operating statements, tax bills and utility bills.
  • Termination of the related-party management agreement and the owner's free basement use at closing.
  • Phase I ESA, building condition assessment (roof, RTUs, elevator, sump/drainage), and zoning compliance letter.

Risk register and diligence checklist

  • HighAdditional-rent clause. Is $17/sf fixed, or a reconciled proportionate share? About $86K/yr is at risk if tenants can reconcile it.
  • HighPharmacy (104) lease expiring 2026, $67K gross and 13% of income. Has the renewal option been exercised? Get written confirmation.
  • HighSecond-floor lease-up is 6,145 sf in a market asking $8–12 net. The value swings by about ±$270K per $3/sf of office rent (see the sensitivity table).
  • HighCapital condition. The roof and rooftop HVAC are original to about 2003. Get a BCA, and price the gap against the $250K allowance.
  • MediumCash tenancies with no lease (B101, B103, B111; $37K/yr). Formalize or vacate them. Check how the cleaner's storage relates to the $22K cleaning contract.
  • MediumSchool (203) expires 2027 at $30.39 gross against about $22 market, a roll-down of about $22K/yr.
  • MediumUtilities of $60.5K are landlord-paid. Check whether units are separately metered and whether the leases allow recovery.
  • MediumTenant identities. The appraisal lists different tenants for 101 and 107. Confirm the covenants, assignments and guarantees.
  • MediumProperty tax reassessment risk. Tax is based on a 2016 assessment that remains frozen. When Ontario reassesses, the tax could rise.

Method notes and limitations
  • All rents are modeled as gross (base + additional rent), and the landlord pays every cost. This keeps the in-place leases, which charge a flat $17, comparable with net-plus-TMI market comps.
  • Market-rent area: existing tenants keep their billed area; new leases use the AutoCAD-measured area. No load factor is added.
  • Stabilized vacancy and credit loss: 5% main floor, 12% office, 10% basement. The blended cap rate is value-weighted across the streams, with costs allocated in proportion to EGI.
  • Lease expiries are assumed at December 31 of the stated end year. The rent roll gives years only.
  • Insurance ($24K), the R&M normalization, the reserve and the $250K capital allowance are estimates pending real quotes and a BCA.
  • Several listing sites block automated access, so some comp details come from search-result summaries. Confirm them with a broker's MLS/CoStar pull before relying on them.
